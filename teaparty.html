PUT TO THE TABLE

Riddles

tap to confess — the Hatter never does

RIDDLE I · THE HATTER

Why is a raven like a writing-desk?

TAP TO CONFESS

The Hatter never answers — the party simply moves on to the next cup. Years later Carroll offered his own: because each can produce a few flat notes — and “nevar” is “raven” spelled backwards.

RIDDLE II · THE DORMOUSE

What lived in the treacle-well?

TAP TO CONFESS

Three sisters — Elsie, Lacie and Tillie — living on treacle, learning to draw everything that begins with an M: mousetraps, the moon, memory, and muchness.

RIDDLE III · TABLE LOGIC

Is “I see what I eat” the same as “I eat what I see”?

TAP TO CONFESS

The Hatter says you might as well claim “I like what I get” is “I get what I like” — and the March Hare adds breathing and sleeping to the wreckage. Alice concedes the point.

RIDDLE IV · THE CLOCK

Why is it always six o'clock?

TAP TO CONFESS

The Hatter sang for the Queen of Hearts and murdered the time — so Time won't do a thing he asks. Stuck at tea-time, the party just moves round the table staining clean cups.

“No room! No room!”— cried all three, though the table was large

TABLE MANNERS

How to Behave

you won't — nobody does

Do not ask for wine: there isn't any, though the Hatter will offer it. Do not cut your bread with the wrong knife, do not say what you mean backwards, and never — ever — suggest washing the cups between whiles.

The Dormouse tells its story only when pinched awake, the March Hare dips its watch in tea to spite the butter, and the whole affair ends the only way it could: Alice leaves in disgust, and they try to stuff the Dormouse into the teapot.

The watch says: butter won't suit the works, the best butter is still wrong, and your hair wants cutting. Take the verdict, keep the thimble, and mind the treacle.

“Have some wine,” said the March Hare in an encouraging tone — and there was none to be had.
