PREFACE

Why This Book

told on a boat, printed for everyone

On a golden afternoon in 1862, an Oxford mathematics lecturer named Charles Dodgson rowed up the Thames telling a story to three sisters — and the middle one, Alice Liddell, demanded it be written down. Three years later it was a book: Alice's Adventures in Wonderland, with ninety-odd engravings by Punch cartoonist John Tenniel.

Because both men died over a century ago, the whole of it — every chapter, every joke, every line Tenniel cut — belongs to everyone now. This archive is built entirely from those free materials: the real text, the real plates, nothing borrowed.

THE END?
